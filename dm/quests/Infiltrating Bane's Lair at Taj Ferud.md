## The Hall of the Fractured Reflection (The Grand Audience & Dining Chamber)

**Atmosphere & Architecture** This expansive, vaulted marble hall serves as the Rakshasa’s semi-public workspace and banquet room. Towering white marble pillars inlaid with lapis lazuli, carnelian, and malachite support a soaring dome painted with gold-leaf constellations. Along the walls hang massive, gilded mirrors that look pristine at a glance, though anyone inspecting them closely notices their reflection lags behind their actual movements by a fraction of a second.

A low, ornate teakwood dining table sits at the center of the room, surrounded by silk cushions and gold-plated hookahs. At the far end of the hall, three wide marble steps lead up to a velvet-draped dais bearing a high-backed ivory throne. The air is thick with the scent of sandalwood, spiced wine, and roasted meats, masking the faint, coppery undertone of old blood.

**Interactive Features & Hazards**

- **The Lagging Mirrors (Magical Hazard):** There are six 10-foot-tall mirrors lining the walls (AC 13, 25 HP, immunity to poison and psychic damage). As a bonus action, the Rakshasa can step into one mirror and teleport out of any other mirror in the room. Additionally, once per round when a creature within 5 feet of a mirror misses the Rakshasa with an attack, the Rakshasa can use its reaction to force the attacker's reflection to lash out, dealing 2d8 slashing damage.
    
- **The Spiced Feast:** The table is laden with opulent delicacies. Any non-fiend who eats or drinks from the table must succeed on a DC 16 Constitution saving throw or be affected as though by the _suggestion_ spell (the Rakshasa telepathically implants a subtle urge to trust its words or hesitate in combat) for 1 hour.
    
- **Hidden Weaponry:** Beneath the silk cushions around the dining table are concealed compartments containing poisoned daggers and vials of _midnight tears_ (usable by the Rakshasa’s cultist attendants or bodyguards).
    

**Encounter Setup & Tactics** The Rakshasa rarely fights alone here. It usually hosts 2 to 4 **Assassin** or **Cult Fanatic** bodyguards disguised as servants, musicians, or minor nobles. The Rakshasa begins encounters seated on the dais, using _dominate person_ or _suggestion_ to turn the party's martial front-liners against their allies before using the mirrors to stay out of reach of melee combatants.

## The Sanctum of Quiet Indulgence (Private Bedchamber & Bath)

**Atmosphere & Architecture** Accessible via a pair of heavy, soundproofed bronze doors behind the dais, this room is a sensory paradise designed for rest and decadence. Delicate marble latticework (_jali_ screens) filters the light into intricate geometric patterns across plush crimson carpets. The northern half of the room features a sunken, steaming marble bath fed by sculpted tiger-head spouts, with water infused with lotus oils and rose petals.

The southern half holds a massive canopy bed draped in sheer black silk, surrounded by low tables stacked with rare books, silver trays of sweetmeats, and a rack of tailored noble garments. A roaring brazier of black iron sits near the bath, burning imported incense that creates a light, hazy fog near the floor.

**Interactive Features & Hazards**

- **The Lotus Bath:** The water is enchanted to soothe the Rakshasa’s fiendish essence. If the Rakshasa starts its turn submerged in the bath, it regains 15 hit points. Any non-fiend entering the water must make a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw or suffer the effects of the _slow_ spell until the end of their next turn as overwhelming lethargy sets in.
    
- **The _Jali_ Screens:** The carved marble dividers provide half cover (+2 AC and Dexterity saving throws) against ranged attacks, and their intricate shadows grant advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made to hide within the chamber.
    
- **Wardrobe of Masks:** A cedar chest at the foot of the bed contains three _hats of disguise_, several sets of fine clothes belonging to prominent local officials, and a ledger detailing blackmail material on the city’s ruling council (DC 17 Investigation check to find the false bottom).
    

**Encounter Setup & Tactics** If cornered here, the Rakshasa uses _major image_ to create duplicates of itself stepping out of the steam, slipping behind the _jali_ screens to cast _plane shift_ as a last resort—or using _fly_ to hover near the vaulted ceiling while peppering intruders with _magic missile_ or enchanted crossbows kept near the bed.

## The Vault of Inverted Truths (Private Study & Trophy Room)

**Atmosphere & Architecture** Concealed behind a secret rotating wall panel in the bedchamber (DC 18 Investigation check, or opened automatically by speaking the Rakshasa's true name backward), this circular, windowless study is where the fiend conducts its true business. The walls are lined with dark mahogany bookshelves packed with forbidden tomes, infernal contracts, and jars containing preserved body parts of defeated rivals.

In the center of the room stands a massive obsidian desk cluttered with silver astrolabes, blood-ink quills, and a crystal orb that displays real-time views of key locations throughout the palace. Behind the desk hangs a gruesome tapestry woven from hair and gold thread, depicting the Rakshasa’s past incarnations tearing apart celestial beings.

**Interactive Features & Hazards**

- **The Inverted Glyphs:** The floor is inlaid with a geometric mosaic of gold wire. Because Rakshasas are inherently reversed in nature (exemplified by their backward hands), any spell cast within this room that requires a spell attack roll has disadvantage, and any saving throw forced by a spell inside this room is made with advantage—except for spells cast by the Rakshasa.
    
- **The Scrying Orb:** A creature using an action to touch the obsidian desk’s crystal orb can cast _scrying_ (DC 17) on anyone currently inside the palace without expending a spell slot.
    
- **Lair Treasure & Contracts:** A locked iron chest under the desk (DC 20 Thieves' Tools check, trapped with a _symbol_ of pain, DC 18) holds 4,500 gp, six uncut rubies worth 500 gp each, a _ring of mind shielding_, and three signed infernal contracts binding local merchants and nobles to the Rakshasa's will.
